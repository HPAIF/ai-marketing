# Research behind the AI marketing member

What an early-stage B2B startup needs from marketing, and the established frameworks the rules
and skills rely on. Short paraphrases with links; read the sources for detail. Researched
2026-11-22. Secondary summaries are marked as such — prefer the original books and pages.

## What marketing should do at an early-stage B2B startup

- Practitioner guides agree on the order: write down the ICP and positioning first and get the
  founder aligned on them, then pick a small number of channels where the ICP already spends
  time; content (search and thought leadership) and LinkedIn are the usual B2B starting points,
  and founder-led content works well early.
  [MarketerHire — startup marketing playbook](https://marketerhire.com/blog/startup-marketing-playbook) ·
  [MarketerHire — founding marketer](https://marketerhire.com/blog/founding-marketer)
- The first marketing work is usually product marketing (positioning, messaging, launches) plus
  demand generation; brand work matters less until scale.
  [Sierra Ventures — first head of marketing](https://www.sierraventures.com/ascend/hiring-your-first-head-of-marketing) ·
  [Stage 2 Capital — first B2B marketer](https://www.stage2.capital/blog/hiring-your-first-b2b-marketer) ·
  [First Round Review — Lattice's first marketing hire](https://review.firstround.com/passing-the-button-clicker-test-and-other-0-to-1-lessons-from-lattices-first-marketing-hire/)

**Used for:** the scope (ICP → positioning → content and copy → launches → channels) and the
order of the first obligations created by `setup`.

## ICP and personas

- The ICP describes the *account* (firmographics such as industry, size, location, business
  model, plus tech stack and buying signals); personas describe the *people* in the deal (role,
  pains, authority, preferences). Build the ICP first from your best actual customers, keep it
  narrow, then add personas.
  [Close — ICP vs. buyer persona](https://close.com/blog/ideal-customer-profile-vs-buyer-persona) ·
  [ZoomInfo — ideal customer profile](https://pipeline.zoominfo.com/marketing/ideal-customer-profile)

**Used in:** `icp-personas`, `icp.md` (the shared file sales reads).

## Jobs to be done and customer interviews

- Switch interviews rebuild the story of a purchase the customer already made — from the first
  thought that something had to change to the decision — to find the real job, its pushes,
  pulls and anxieties. Messaging that speaks to functional, emotional and social sides of the job
  lands better than feature lists. (Mostly practitioner sources.)
  [Business of Software — JTBD in marketing](https://businessofsoftware.org/jobs-to-be-done-in-marketing/) ·
  [CleverX — how to run JTBD interviews](https://cleverx.com/blog/how-to-run-jobs-to-be-done-interviews)
- *The Mom Test* (Rob Fitzpatrick): talk about the customer's life, not your idea; ask about
  specific past behaviour, not hypothetical future behaviour; listen more than you talk.
  [Shortform — what is the Mom Test](https://www.shortform.com/blog/what-is-the-mom-test/) ·
  [mtlynch — book report](https://mtlynch.io/book-reports/the-mom-test/)

**Used in:** `icp-personas` (jobs table, interview guide), `positioning` (customers' words).

## Positioning — April Dunford

- Positioning defines how a product is the best at something a specific set of customers care
  about. Dunford's components, worked in this order: competitive alternatives (including doing
  nothing) → differentiated capabilities → the value they create → the customers who care most →
  the market category that makes that value obvious (a relevant trend can be layered on). She
  advises against fill-in-the-blank positioning statements, against competing with alternatives
  customers don't really consider, and against inventing a new category too early.
  [Lenny's Newsletter — a quickstart guide to positioning](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/positioning) ·
  [Heinz Marketing — five components of effective positioning (summary)](https://www.heinzmarketing.com/blog/five-components-of-effective-positioning-an-obviously-awesome-book-summary-part-2/) ·
  [April Dunford — books](https://aprildunford.com/books)

**Used in:** `positioning`, `positioning.md`.

## Messaging — StoryBrand (SB7)

- Donald Miller's seven-part story: a character (the customer, as hero) has a problem, meets a
  guide (the company) who gives a plan and calls them to action, which helps them avoid failure
  and end in success. Applied through a one-page "BrandScript". (Secondary summaries.)
  [Shortform — the SB7 formula](https://www.shortform.com/blog/donald-miller-building-a-storybrand/) ·
  [Impact — what is the StoryBrand framework](https://www.impactplus.com/blog/what-is-the-storybrand-framework)

**Used in:** `positioning` (story table), `copywriting` (website and landing structure).

## Copy flow — AIDA

- Attention → interest → desire → action: a sequence that grew out of early-20th-century
  advertising and sales writing; usually credited to E. St. Elmo Lewis (1898), though the history
  is disputed and variants exist.
  [Wikipedia — AIDA (marketing)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AIDA_(marketing)) ·
  [Rework — history of the AIDA model](https://resources.rework.com/libraries/history-of-sales/history-of-the-aida-model) ·
  [HubSpot — AIDA model](https://blog.hubspot.com/marketing/aida-model)

**Used in:** `copywriting` (flow of a piece), `content-plan` (balance across stages).

## Channels — the Bullseye framework

- From *Traction* (Gabriel Weinberg & Justin Mares): brainstorm across 19 traction channels, rank
  them into rings (possible / promising / test now), run cheap tests on the best few, then focus
  on the one channel that works.
  [Gabriel Weinberg — the Bullseye framework](https://medium.com/@yegg/the-bullseye-framework-for-getting-traction-ef49d05bfd7e) ·
  [Brian Balfour — strategize, test, measure](https://brianbalfour.com/essays/traction-the-bullseye-framework) ·
  [Rework — Bullseye framework](https://resources.rework.com/libraries/growth-frameworks/bullseye-framework)

**Used in:** `channel-review`, `channels.md`, `content-plan` (channels to use).

## Launches — launch tiers

- Tier a launch by expected impact before planning it: tier 1 for new products or markets
  (full campaign), tier 2 for significant features (lighter), tier 3 for small changes (release
  notes, customer notice). Tier definitions and timelines vary by source; when unsure, default
  to tier 2.
  [Pragmatic Institute — launch tiers](https://www.pragmaticinstitute.com/resources/articles/product/prioritize-product-launch-resources-with-launch-tiers/) ·
  [Product Marketing Alliance — launch tiers framework](https://www.productmarketingalliance.com/product-launch-tiers-framework/)

**Used in:** `launch-plan`, `templates/launch-plan.md`.

## Truthful claims, testimonials and marketing consent (why the lawyer checks)

These are examples of the kind of rules that apply; the rules of the company's own target market
are the lawyer member's job.

- US: the FTC Endorsement Guides (16 CFR Part 255, revised 2023) — a real testimonial can still
  imply a performance claim the advertiser must substantiate; results shown should be typical or
  the typical result disclosed; material connections must be disclosed. A separate FTC rule
  targets fake reviews and testimonials.
  [FTC — Endorsement Guides](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/topics/truth-advertising/advertisement-endorsements) ·
  [eCFR — 16 CFR Part 255](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-255)
- US: CAN-SPAM applies to business-to-business email too — accurate headers and subject lines,
  identification as an ad, a postal address, and a working opt-out honoured promptly.
  [FTC — CAN-SPAM compliance guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business)
- UK/EU: data-protection law applies to B2B marketing whenever personal data is processed;
  electronic marketing has its own consent rules.
  [ICO — business-to-business marketing](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/business-to-business-marketing/)

**Used in:** hard rules 2–3, `copywriting` (claims check), `claims.md`.
